**The main difference: a DSCR loan qualifies on the property's rent, a bank statement loan qualifies on your income from bank deposits, and a conventional loan qualifies on your tax returns and debt-to-income ratio. If your tax returns tell the full story, conventional often costs less. If they don't, DSCR or bank statement loans can fill the gap.**

## Key Takeaways

- **DSCR loan:** The property's rent ÷ PITIA. No personal income docs.
- **Bank statement loan:** Income from 12 to 24 months of bank deposits. Still uses debt-to-income (DTI).
- **Conventional investment loan:** Tax returns, W-2s, and DTI. Often the lowest cost when you qualify.
- Fannie Mae caps borrowers at **10 financed properties** for investment purchases.
- Many investors use **more than one type** as their portfolio grows.

## How Does Each Investor Loan Qualify You?

### DSCR loan

A **DSCR (debt service coverage ratio) loan** asks one question: does the rent cover the payment? The lender divides monthly rent by monthly PITIA (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and association dues). Walk through the math in [How to Calculate DSCR](/posts/how-to-calculate-dscr/).

### Bank statement loan

A **bank statement loan** is a **non-QM** loan for self-employed borrowers. Instead of tax returns, the lender reviews **12 or 24 months** of personal or business bank statements. It totals deposits, applies an expense factor for business accounts, and calculates your income. Then it checks your **DTI**, just like a regular loan.

### Conventional investment loan

A **conventional loan** follows Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac rules. You document income with W-2s, pay stubs, or tax returns. For a rental purchase, Fannie Mae generally counts **75% of the gross rent** toward income ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-3.8-02](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.8-02/rental-income-subject-property)). Everything goes into your DTI.

## DSCR vs. Bank Statement vs. Conventional: Side by Side

| | DSCR loan | Bank statement loan | Conventional investment loan |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Qualifies on** | Property rent vs. payment | Your deposits (12-24 mo.) | Tax returns, W-2s, DTI |
| **Personal income docs** | Usually none | Bank statements | Full documentation |
| **DTI ratio** | Usually not used | Yes | Yes |
| **Close in an LLC** | Commonly allowed | Varies | Generally not (title in your name) |
| **Typical down payment** | Often 20%-25%+ | Varies by program | As low as 15% on a 1-unit purchase* |
| **Property count limit** | Varies by lender | Varies by lender | Up to 10 financed properties* |
| **Pricing** | Non-QM; often higher than conventional | Non-QM; often higher than conventional | Often the lowest |

*Per Fannie Mae's [eligibility matrix](https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/media/document/pdf/eligibility-matrix-current) and [Selling Guide B2-2-03](https://guide-selling.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-2-03/multiple-financed-properties-same-borrower). DSCR and bank statement terms shown are typical industry ranges that vary by lender, not Coastal Funding program terms.

## When Is a DSCR Loan the Best Fit?

A DSCR loan often makes sense when:

- You're **self-employed** and your tax returns show low income after write-offs.
- You want to **close in an LLC** to keep rentals separate.
- You already have **many financed properties** and are near conventional limits.
- You want a **faster, simpler file** with fewer income documents.
- The property's **rent comfortably covers** the payment.

## When Is a Bank Statement Loan the Best Fit?

Bank statement loans are worth a look when:

- You're self-employed with **strong, steady deposits**.
- The rental's **DSCR is low**, but your own income is strong enough to carry it.
- You're buying a **primary home or second home** too. DSCR loans are for investment properties only.

## When Is a Conventional Loan the Best Fit?

Conventional is often the lower-cost choice when:

- Your **tax returns show enough income** to qualify.
- You own **fewer than 10 financed properties**.
- You're fine holding title in **your own name**.
- You want the **smallest down payment** on a one-unit rental.

Keep in mind that Fannie Mae requires more reserves and higher credit as your number of financed properties grows. With 7 to 10 financed properties, borrowers need at least a **720 credit score** ([Fannie Mae B2-2-03](https://guide-selling.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-2-03/multiple-financed-properties-same-borrower)).

## Can You Use More Than One Loan Type?

Yes, and many investors do. A common path looks like this:

1. Buy the first few rentals with **conventional** loans while your DTI allows.
2. Switch to **DSCR** loans as write-offs, DTI, or property count start to limit you.
3. Use a **DSCR cash-out refinance** to pull equity from a stabilized property. See our [DSCR cash-out refinance and BRRRR guide](/posts/dscr-cash-out-refinance-brrrr/).
