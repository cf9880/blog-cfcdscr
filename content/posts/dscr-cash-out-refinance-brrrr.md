**A DSCR cash-out refinance replaces the loan on a rental property with a larger one and gives you the difference in cash. It qualifies mainly on the property's rent compared with the new payment, not your personal income. That's why it's a popular "refinance" step in the BRRRR strategy: Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat.**

## Key Takeaways

- **Cash-out** = new loan − old loan payoff − closing costs.
- DSCR lenders often cap cash-out around **70% to 75% loan-to-value**. It varies by lender.
- **Seasoning** rules decide whether the lender uses the new appraised value or your cost.
- Many DSCR programs let you **close in an LLC**, usually with a personal guarantee.
- The new loan still needs a solid **DSCR**, so check the rent against the new payment.

## How Does a DSCR Cash-Out Refinance Work?

1. You apply with the property's lease or expected rent.
2. An appraiser sets the **current value** and **market rent**.
3. The lender sets a **max loan** based on loan-to-value (LTV) and the DSCR on the new payment.
4. The new loan pays off the old one. You keep what's left after closing costs.

**LTV** means loan amount ÷ property value. A 75% LTV on a $300,000 home is a $225,000 loan.

## What Is the BRRRR Strategy?

**BRRRR** stands for:

- **Buy** a property below its potential value, often with cash or a short-term loan
- **Rehab** it to add value
- **Rent** it to a qualified tenant
- **Refinance** into long-term financing, often a DSCR loan, to pull cash back out
- **Repeat** with the next property

The refinance step is where a DSCR loan shines. You don't have to prove more personal income every time you add a door.

### BRRRR example

These numbers are made up to show the math. They're not quotes.

- Purchase price: **$160,000** (cash)
- Rehab: **$40,000**
- **Total in: $200,000**
- After-repair appraised value: **$280,000**
- Market rent: **$2,300**

At a 75% LTV, the max loan would be **$210,000**. If closing costs are $7,000, you'd get back about **$203,000**, more than you put in.

But the DSCR still has to work. If the new PITIA is $1,950, then $2,300 ÷ $1,950 = **1.18 DSCR**. If the payment were higher than the rent, the lender might cut the loan amount.

Not sure how to run the ratio? See [How to Calculate DSCR](/posts/how-to-calculate-dscr/).

## What Are Seasoning Requirements?

**Seasoning** is how long you've owned the property before you refinance. It's one of the biggest variables between lenders:

- Some DSCR lenders allow cash-out using the **new appraised value** after a few months of ownership.
- Others use the **lower of the appraised value or your purchase price plus documented rehab costs** until you've owned it longer, often 6 to 12 months.
- Keep **receipts, invoices, and permits** for your rehab. They help prove the value you added.

Conventional loans have stricter rules. Fannie Mae requires an existing first mortgage to be at least **12 months old** (note date to note date) for a cash-out refinance. Its **delayed financing** exception allows cash-out within six months of a **cash purchase**, but the new loan generally can't exceed what you actually invested plus closing costs ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide B2-1.3-03](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-1.3-03/cash-out-refinance-transactions)). That cap can limit BRRRR returns, which is one reason investors look at DSCR. Compare all your options in [DSCR vs. Bank Statement vs. Conventional](/posts/dscr-vs-bank-statement-loan/).

## Can You Close a DSCR Loan in an LLC?

Many DSCR programs allow **LLC vesting**, meaning the LLC is the borrower on title. Here's what investors commonly see:

- The LLC's **operating agreement, articles of organization, and EIN letter**
- A **personal guarantee** from members who own a set share of the LLC
- Credit checks on the guarantors
- Some lenders allow **multi-member LLCs**; rules vary

If you bought the property in your personal name, ask whether you can move title to your LLC as part of the refinance. Talk to your attorney and tax pro about liability and tax effects.

## What Should You Watch Before You Refinance?

- **Prepayment penalties.** Many DSCR loans have them, often stepping down over a few years. Make sure the term fits your plan.
- **Rate vs. cash.** Pulling more cash means a bigger payment and a lower DSCR.
- **Reserves.** Lenders usually want several months of PITIA in the bank after closing.
- **Market rates.** Freddie Mac's 30-year average was **7.32%** on October 22, 2026, versus **6.19%** a year earlier ([Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)). DSCR pricing is separate, but higher rates mean bigger payments. See our [Fall 2026 DSCR rate update](/posts/dscr-loan-rates-investors-2026/).
