**Yes, you can often use a DSCR loan for a condo, including many non-warrantable condos that conventional agency loans can't finance. The lender looks mainly at whether the unit's rent covers its full monthly payment, including HOA dues, and whether the project meets that lender's condo guidelines.**

Condos can be an affordable way into rental investing, especially in beach and resort markets. But condo financing has extra rules. Before you write an offer this holiday season or plan your 2027 purchases, here's how DSCR loans for condos work, what "non-warrantable" means, and how to check a building before you buy.

## Key Takeaways

- **DSCR loans qualify the property's rent,** not your personal income, and many lenders finance condos.
- **Non-warrantable condos** don't meet Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac project standards, so agency loans aren't available.
- **Fannie Mae's ineligible list** (Selling Guide B4-2.1-03, updated Nov. 4, 2026) includes hotel-style operations, over 35% commercial space, and heavy single-owner concentration.
- **HOA dues count in your DSCR.** High dues can push a deal below a lender's minimum ratio.
- **Do your homework on the HOA:** budget, reserves, litigation, insurance, and rental rules.

## What Is a DSCR Loan?

**DSCR** stands for debt service coverage ratio. It divides a property's monthly rent by its monthly housing payment, called **PITIA**: principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and association (HOA) dues. A DSCR of 1.0 means rent exactly covers the payment. Each lender sets its own minimum. Learn the math in our guide on [how to calculate DSCR](/posts/how-to-calculate-dscr/).

DSCR loans are business-purpose loans for investment properties only, not owner-occupied homes.

## What Is a Non-Warrantable Condo?

A **warrantable condo** is a unit in a project that meets Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac project standards, so it can be financed with a conventional agency loan. A **non-warrantable condo** is in a project that doesn't meet those standards.

Fannie Mae's [Selling Guide B4-2.1-03, Ineligible Projects](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b4-2.1-03/ineligible-projects) (updated November 4, 2026) lists traits that make a project ineligible, including:

- **Hotel or motel operations.** Projects managed and operated like a hotel, often called condotels.
- **Too much commercial space.** Nonresidential or commercial space above 35% of the project.
- **Single-entity ownership.** One owner holding more than 20% of units in a project with 21 or more units, or more than two units in a project with 11 to 20 units.
- **Pending litigation** involving the HOA, or developer litigation tied to safety, structural soundness, habitability, or functional use.
- **Critical repairs,** including material deficiencies and significant deferred maintenance.
- **Mandatory outside memberships,** such as required fees for a golf or country club owned by an outside party.
- **Timeshare or fractional ownership** projects.

Non-warrantable doesn't mean a bad investment. It means you'll need a lender that keeps the loan on its own books or sells it outside the agency market. That's where many DSCR programs come in.

## How Do HOA Dues Change Your DSCR?

HOA dues are often the biggest surprise for condo investors. Here's an illustrative example:

| Monthly item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Principal and interest | $1,450 |
| Property taxes | $250 |
| HO-6 insurance (unit policy) | $100 |
| HOA dues | $450 |
| **Total PITIA** | **$2,250** |
| Market rent | $2,300 |
| **DSCR** | **1.02** |

*Example is illustrative only, not a quote.* If the HOA raises dues by $150 a month, PITIA becomes $2,400, and the DSCR drops to about 0.96. That could change your rate, your down payment, or whether the loan works at all. Always ask for the current budget and any planned dues increases or special assessments.

The appraiser's rent estimate matters too. See our guide to the [DSCR appraisal rent schedule (Form 1007)](/posts/dscr-appraisal-rent-schedule-form-1007/).

## What Should You Check Before Buying a Rental Condo?

1. **HOA questionnaire.** Lenders use it to review owner-occupancy, delinquencies, litigation, and commercial space.
2. **Budget and reserves.** Low reserves can signal future special assessments.
3. **Inspections and repairs.** Some states, including Florida, have added condo inspection and reserve rules in recent years. Ask for recent inspection and reserve study reports.
4. **Master insurance policy.** Confirm coverage, deductibles, and premium trends.
5. **Rental restrictions.** Many HOAs set minimum lease terms or cap rentals. This matters for nightly rentals. Read our [DSCR loan for short-term rentals](/posts/dscr-loan-short-term-rental/) guide.
6. **Litigation and special assessments.** Ask directly, in writing, before the end of your inspection period.

## How Do DSCR Condo Terms Compare With Single-Family?

Terms vary by lender, but non-warrantable and condotel loans often call for a larger down payment, higher credit scores, or more reserves than a typical single-family DSCR loan. Some lenders also limit unit size or require a minimum number of units in the project. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use a DSCR loan to buy a condo?

Often yes. Many DSCR lenders finance condo units, including some non-warrantable condos, as long as the rent supports the payment and the project meets that lender's guidelines.

### What is a non-warrantable condo?

It is a condo unit in a project that doesn't meet Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac project standards, so a conventional agency loan isn't available. Common reasons include pending structural litigation, too much commercial space, or one owner holding too many units.

### What makes a condo project ineligible for Fannie Mae?

Fannie Mae's Selling Guide B4-2.1-03 lists ineligible traits such as hotel-style operation, commercial space above 35%, single-entity ownership above 20% in projects with 21 or more units, critical repairs, and certain pending litigation.

### Do HOA dues affect my DSCR?

Yes. HOA dues are part of PITIA, the monthly payment used in the DSCR formula. Higher dues lower your ratio, so check the HOA budget before you make an offer.

### Are condotels eligible for DSCR loans?

Some DSCR lenders offer condotel programs, often with larger down payments and stricter terms. Fannie Mae does not buy loans on projects operated as hotels or motels.
