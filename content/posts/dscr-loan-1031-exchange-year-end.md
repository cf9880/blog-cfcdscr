**A DSCR loan can finance the replacement property in a 1031 exchange because it qualifies you on the property's rent, not your tax returns. If you sell a rental late in 2026, check your dates carefully: the 180-day window can end early, on your tax return due date, unless you file an extension.**

Fall is a common time to sell a rental and trade into something better. Here's how the IRS timeline works for sales in the last months of 2026, and how to line up DSCR financing so the loan doesn't become the reason an exchange fails.

## Key Takeaways

- **Two deadlines:** identify replacement property within **45 days**, and receive it by the **earlier** of 180 days or your tax return due date, including extensions.
- **Late-2026 sales can lose days.** If you sell after about October 17, 2026, day 180 lands after April 15, 2027, so an extension may be needed to keep the full window.
- **Real property only.** Since 2018, only real property qualifies for like-kind exchange treatment.
- **Use a qualified intermediary.** If you take control of the sale cash, the exchange can fail.
- **DSCR fits exchanges well:** the loan is underwritten on rent, so you can start financing before the sale closes.

## How Do the 1031 Exchange Deadlines Work?

Under the IRS rules for like-kind exchanges, you must **identify** the replacement property in writing within 45 days of transferring the property you sold. You must then **receive** the replacement property by the earlier of:

1. 180 days after the transfer, or
2. the due date, including extensions, of your tax return for the year of the transfer.

These deadlines are strict. They generally can't be extended because a loan or closing runs late; limited relief exists mainly for federally declared disasters ([IRS Form 8824 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8824)).

## Why Do Fall Sales Need Extra Planning?

For most individual investors, the 2026 return is due **April 15, 2027**. For any sale after about mid-October 2026, day 180 falls later than that, so the tax return deadline comes first unless you extend.

| Sale (transfer) date | Day 45 (identify by) | Day 180 | Tax-return limit without extension |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oct. 17, 2026 | Dec. 1, 2026 | Apr. 15, 2027 | Apr. 15, 2027 |
| Oct. 27, 2026 | Dec. 11, 2026 | Apr. 25, 2027 | Apr. 15, 2027 |
| Nov. 15, 2026 | Dec. 30, 2026 | May 14, 2027 | Apr. 15, 2027 |
| Dec. 15, 2026 | Jan. 29, 2027 | June 13, 2027 | Apr. 15, 2027 |
| Dec. 31, 2026 | Feb. 14, 2027 | June 29, 2027 | Apr. 15, 2027 |

*Dates are calendar math for illustration. Confirm your exact deadlines with your qualified intermediary and CPA.*

The fix is usually simple: ask your CPA about filing an extension so you keep the full 180 days. Don't file your return and close the exchange afterward without talking to your tax advisor first.

## What Else Must a 1031 Exchange Follow?

The [IRS like-kind exchange tax tips](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/like-kind-exchanges-real-estate-tax-tips) and Form 8824 instructions highlight a few rules investors often miss:

- **Business or investment use only.** Your primary home doesn't qualify, and neither does property held mainly to resell (like many fix-and-flips).
- **U.S. for U.S.** Property in the United States and property outside it are not like-kind.
- **No touching the money.** A qualified intermediary holds the sale proceeds. Your agent, attorney, or a related party generally can't serve as your intermediary.
- **Boot is taxable.** Cash you keep, and debt relief that isn't replaced, can trigger tax on part of the gain.
- **Related-party rules** can undo the deferral if you trade with family or entities you control and either side sells within two years.

## How Does a DSCR Loan Fit a 1031 Exchange?

A DSCR loan qualifies the replacement property on its own cash flow: monthly rent compared with principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and HOA dues. That helps in an exchange for three reasons:

1. **Speed.** There are no tax returns or W-2s to review, which can shorten the timeline when you have 45 and 180-day clocks running. (See [how to calculate DSCR](/posts/how-to-calculate-dscr/).)
2. **Replacing debt.** To avoid boot, you usually need to buy property of equal or greater value and replace the debt paid off on the sale. New DSCR financing can cover that difference.
3. **Many investors hold property in an LLC.** Many DSCR lenders lend to LLCs. Ask your QI and CPA how to title the new property so the same taxpayer sells and buys.

Typical DSCR guidelines vary by lender. Common ranges include a credit score around 620–700+, a 20%–25% down payment, a DSCR of about 1.0 or higher, and several months of reserves. Read our [DSCR loan requirements](/posts/dscr-loan-requirements/) for the details.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use a DSCR loan for a 1031 exchange?

Yes. Many investors use a DSCR loan to finance the replacement property. The loan qualifies on the property's rent, and the exchange funds from your qualified intermediary go toward the down payment and closing costs.

### What are the 1031 exchange deadlines?

You must identify replacement property within 45 days of the sale, and receive it by the earlier of 180 days or your tax return due date, including extensions, per IRS Form 8824 instructions.

### Why does a late-2026 sale have a shorter exchange window?

For a sale after about October 17, 2026, day 180 comes after the April 15, 2027 tax filing date. Unless you extend your return, the exchange must close by that filing date.

### Do I have to replace my old mortgage in a 1031 exchange?

Debt paid off that isn't replaced by new debt or added cash can be treated as taxable boot. Your CPA can run the numbers. New DSCR financing is one way to replace debt.

### Can I close a 1031 exchange property in an LLC?

Often yes, but titling must keep the same taxpayer on both sides. A single-member LLC may be treated differently than a partnership. Confirm with your QI and CPA before closing.

### How fast can a DSCR loan close?

It varies by lender, appraisal, and title. Starting the loan before your sale closes and ordering the appraisal right after you're under contract helps you stay within the deadlines.
