**To calculate DSCR for a rental property loan, divide the property's gross monthly rent by its monthly PITIA (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and HOA dues). A result of 1.0 means the rent exactly covers the payment. Many DSCR lenders want 1.0 or higher, and stronger ratios often get better pricing.**

New to DSCR loans? Start with our [welcome guide](/posts/welcome/). This post goes deeper into the math, so you can run the numbers before you make an offer.

## Key Takeaways

- **DSCR formula:** gross monthly rent ÷ monthly PITIA.
- **1.0** = break-even. **Above 1.0** = the rent covers the payment with room to spare.
- Many lenders price better around **1.20 to 1.25+**. Some allow **below 1.0** with tighter terms. It varies by lender.
- Lenders usually use the **lower of the lease or market rent**.
- You can raise your DSCR with **more down payment, lower insurance, or higher rent**.

## What Is DSCR?

**DSCR** stands for **debt service coverage ratio**. It measures whether a property's income covers its debt payment. On a residential DSCR loan, the lender cares most about this one number instead of your W-2s or tax returns.

**PITIA** is the full monthly housing payment:

- **P**rincipal
- **I**nterest
- **T**axes (property taxes)
- **I**nsurance (hazard, plus flood if required)
- **A**ssociation dues (HOA or condo fees)

## How Do You Calculate DSCR, Step by Step?

### Step 1: Find the qualifying rent

Lenders commonly use the **lower** of:

- The **signed lease**, if the property is rented, or
- The **market rent** from the appraiser's rent schedule (often Fannie Mae Form 1007 for a single-family home).

For a vacant property you're buying, the appraiser's market-rent estimate is usually the number that counts.

### Step 2: Add up the full PITIA

Get real quotes, not guesses. Insurance and taxes can change a deal fast. Ask the county for the tax figure and get an insurance quote early.

### Step 3: Divide

**DSCR = Monthly rent ÷ Monthly PITIA**

## DSCR Calculation Examples

These are made-up examples to show the math, not quotes or offers.

**Example 1: Single-family rental**

- Market rent: **$2,100**
- Principal and interest: $1,320
- Taxes: $210
- Insurance: $140
- HOA: $0
- **PITIA: $1,670**

$2,100 ÷ $1,670 = **1.26 DSCR**. Rent covers the payment with about 26% to spare.

**Example 2: Same house, bigger insurance bill**

If insurance jumps to $390 a month, PITIA becomes $1,920.

$2,100 ÷ $1,920 = **1.09 DSCR**. Still above 1.0, but in a lower tier at many lenders.

**Example 3: Duplex**

- Unit A rent: $1,350
- Unit B rent: $1,300
- **Total rent: $2,650**
- **PITIA: $2,400**

$2,650 ÷ $2,400 = **1.10 DSCR**. On a 2-4 unit property, lenders add up the rent from all units.

## What DSCR Do Lenders Want?

Every lender sets its own tiers. Here's how they commonly break down in the market:

| DSCR | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| 1.25 or higher | Strong. Often the best pricing and highest leverage. |
| 1.00 to 1.24 | Rent covers the payment. Widely accepted. |
| Below 1.00 | Rent doesn't fully cover PITIA. Some lenders offer "no-ratio" or low-ratio programs, usually with a bigger down payment, more reserves, or a higher rate. |

These are typical industry ranges, not Coastal Funding program terms. Your options depend on credit, down payment, reserves, property type, and the lender.

## Does DSCR Use Gross Rent or NOI?

Most **residential** DSCR loans (1-4 units) use **gross rent ÷ PITIA**. They don't subtract repairs, vacancy, or management fees.

**Commercial** loans on larger buildings often use **net operating income (NOI) ÷ annual debt service**. That's a stricter test, since NOI subtracts operating expenses first. Always ask which formula your lender uses.

Compare that with a conventional loan. Fannie Mae's Selling Guide generally counts only **75% of gross rent** on a purchase to allow for vacancy and upkeep ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-3.8-02](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.8-02/rental-income-subject-property)). Then that income goes into your personal debt-to-income ratio. A DSCR loan skips the personal income test. See [DSCR vs. bank statement vs. conventional loans](/posts/dscr-vs-bank-statement-loan/) for a full side-by-side.

## How Can You Raise Your DSCR?

If your ratio is a little short, try these levers:

1. **Put more down.** A smaller loan means a smaller payment.
2. **Shop insurance.** Even $100 a month can move you up a tier.
3. **Check for interest-only options.** Some DSCR programs calculate the ratio on an interest-only payment. Availability varies.
4. **Buy down the rate.** Paying points lowers the monthly payment.
5. **Raise rent to market.** If the current lease is below market, a new lease can help, though many lenders still use the lower figure.
6. **Pick a property with lower HOA dues or taxes.**

## Does DSCR Change With Today's Rates?

Yes. Higher rates mean higher principal and interest, which lowers DSCR on the same rent. Freddie Mac's 30-year average was **7.32%** as of October 22, 2026, down from 7.40% a week earlier ([Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)). DSCR loans are priced separately from those conventional averages, but the trend matters. For more, read our [Fall 2026 DSCR rates and rental market update](/posts/dscr-loan-rates-investors-2026/).
