**Before year-end, DSCR rental investors should gather their Form 1098, closing disclosures, rent records, and placed-in-service dates. Per IRS Publication 527, rental mortgage interest is generally deductible, points are generally deducted over the loan term, some loan costs are added to basis, and residential rentals are depreciated over 27.5 years.**

Thanksgiving week is a good time to pull your rental files together before the holiday rush. A **DSCR loan** (debt service coverage ratio loan) qualifies you based on a property's rental income rather than your personal income, but the tax side of owning that rental still runs through your own return or your entity's. This checklist covers the loan-related records your CPA will likely ask for. It is general information, not tax advice.

## Key Takeaways

- **Collect every Form 1098.** You should get one if you paid $600 or more of rental mortgage interest to one lender or servicer.
- **Points aren't usually deducted all at once.** The IRS treats points on a rental loan as prepaid interest deducted over the loan term.
- **Some loan costs go into basis,** including mortgage commissions, abstract fees and recording fees.
- **Depreciation runs 27.5 years** for residential rental buildings under the General Depreciation System. Land isn't depreciable.
- **Your placed-in-service date matters.** Depreciation can begin when the property is ready and available for rent.
- **Ask a CPA** before you make any year-end move only for tax reasons.

## What Loan Records Should DSCR Investors Gather Before Year-End?

Here's a simple checklist for each property:

| Record | Why it matters | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| Form 1098 (Mortgage Interest Statement) | Shows interest paid for the year | Your loan servicer, usually in January |
| Closing Disclosure | Lists points, lender fees, and other loan costs | Your closing package |
| Settlement statement | Shows purchase price and prorations | Title company |
| Rent roll and leases | Supports rental income | Your records or property manager |
| Placed-in-service date | Starts depreciation | Date the unit was ready and available for rent |
| Escrow statement | Property taxes and insurance paid | Your servicer's annual escrow analysis |

If you closed or refinanced a DSCR loan in 2026, keep both the old and new loan documents. Your CPA may need both to sort out interest, points, and payoff costs.

## Is DSCR Loan Interest Deductible?

**Mortgage interest** paid to banks and other lenders is listed among rental expenses in [IRS Publication 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527), *Residential Rental Property*. Rental income and expenses are generally reported on Schedule E (Form 1040), though entity owners may file differently.

A few things to keep in mind:

- **Interest-only DSCR loans** still generate interest each month. Interest-only payments can lower your monthly payment and raise your DSCR, but they don't pay down principal.
- **Loans held in an LLC** may be reported on a business or partnership return. See our [DSCR loan for an LLC guide](/posts/dscr-loan-llc/) for how entity borrowing works.
- **Limits can apply.** The IRS has passive activity and at-risk rules that can limit rental losses. Your CPA can tell you if they affect you.

## How Are Points and Loan Costs Treated?

This is where many investors get surprised.

### Points

**Points** (sometimes called loan origination fees or discount points) are charges paid to get a loan. Per IRS Publication 527, points paid solely for the use of money are interest. Because they are prepaid interest, you generally **can't deduct the full amount in the year paid**. Instead, you deduct them over the term of the loan, using rules the IRS describes in the publication.

That matters for DSCR borrowers who buy down a rate. A buydown may help your monthly cash flow and your DSCR, but the tax benefit is usually spread out.

### Other loan costs

IRS Publication 527 says certain expenses you pay to obtain a mortgage on rental property **can't be deducted as interest**. These include **mortgage commissions, abstract fees, and recording fees**. They're capital expenses that become part of your **basis** in the property.

**Basis** is, in simple terms, your investment in the property for tax purposes. It affects depreciation and your gain or loss when you sell.

## How Does Rental Property Depreciation Work?

**Depreciation** lets you recover the cost of a rental building over time. Under the IRS's General Depreciation System (GDS), residential rental property is depreciated over **27.5 years**.

- **Land isn't depreciable.** The IRS says land generally doesn't wear out, so you need to separate the land value from the building value.
- **Start date:** you can begin depreciating rental property when it is **ready and available for rent**.
- **Mid-month convention:** residential rental property is treated as placed in service at the midpoint of the month it's placed in service.

Your DSCR appraisal can help with records, but it isn't a tax allocation. The appraisal measures value and market rent for the loan, not the land-versus-building split your CPA uses.

## Should You Close a Rental Before December 31?

It depends. Closing in 2026 versus 2027 can change your placed-in-service date, but a December closing doesn't automatically create a 2026 tax benefit. If the unit isn't ready and available for rent, depreciation may not start yet. ## Year-End Checklist: Quick Version

1. **Download or request every Form 1098** for each rental loan.
2. **Pull your Closing Disclosures** for any 2026 purchase or refinance.
3. **List points and lender fees** separately for your CPA.
4. **Note each property's placed-in-service date.**
5. **Update rent rolls and leases** for 2027, which also helps if you plan to refinance or buy.
6. **Calculate your DSCR** on each property with updated rents and expenses. See [how to calculate DSCR](/posts/how-to-calculate-dscr/).
7. **Book time with your CPA** before year-end if you're weighing a December closing or refinance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I deduct DSCR loan interest on a rental property?

Mortgage interest on a rental property is generally a deductible rental expense, according to IRS Publication 527. How it applies to your return depends on your situation, so confirm with a CPA.

### Can I deduct points paid on a DSCR loan in the year I close?

Generally no. IRS Publication 527 says points on a rental property loan are prepaid interest that you generally deduct over the term of the loan, not all in the year paid.

### Are DSCR loan closing costs deductible?

Some aren't deductible as interest. IRS Publication 527 says costs to get a mortgage on rental property, such as mortgage commissions, abstract fees and recording fees, are capital expenses added to your basis.

### How long do you depreciate a rental property?

Under the IRS General Depreciation System, residential rental property is depreciated over 27.5 years. Land can't be depreciated.

### When does depreciation start on a rental property?

IRS Publication 527 says you can begin depreciating rental property when it's ready and available for rent, and residential rental property uses a mid-month convention.

### Will I get a Form 1098 for my DSCR loan?

If you paid $600 or more of mortgage interest on a rental property to one lender or servicer, IRS Publication 527 says you should receive a Form 1098 or similar statement.
