**Yes, an LLC can get a DSCR loan. Many DSCR lenders let investors close in the name of a limited liability company, using the property's rental income instead of personal income to qualify. Most still require the owners to sign a personal guarantee and provide the LLC's formation documents. Conventional Fannie Mae loans, by contrast, generally must be made to individuals.**

As year-end approaches, many investors are setting up entities and planning purchases for 2027. If you want your next rental titled in an LLC, here's how DSCR financing usually works, what lenders ask for, and the mistakes to avoid.

## Key Takeaways

- **DSCR lenders commonly lend to LLCs.** Conventional loans sold to Fannie Mae generally require borrowers to be individuals, not LLCs.
- **Expect a personal guarantee** from the LLC's members in most cases.
- **Have your documents ready:** articles of organization, operating agreement, EIN letter, and a certificate of good standing.
- **Moving an existing financed rental into an LLC** can raise due-on-sale concerns. Talk to your lender and attorney first.
- **DSCR loans are business-purpose loans** for investment properties only, not homes you live in.

## What Is a DSCR Loan?

A **debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) loan** qualifies you based on the property's cash flow. The lender compares the rent to the monthly housing payment:

**DSCR = monthly rent / monthly PITIA**

**PITIA** means principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and association dues. For example, $2,400 in rent divided by a $2,000 PITIA is a 1.20 DSCR. Our guide on [how to calculate DSCR](/posts/how-to-calculate-dscr/) walks through more examples, and our [DSCR loan requirements](/posts/dscr-loan-requirements/) post covers credit, down payment, and reserves.

## Can an LLC Get a Mortgage on a Rental Property?

It depends on the loan type.

- **Conventional loans:** [Fannie Mae's Selling Guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-2-01/general-borrower-eligibility-requirements) lists eligible borrowers as individuals, with limited exceptions such as certain revocable trusts. LLCs, corporations, and partnerships aren't on that list.
- **DSCR loans:** These are typically non-QM, business-purpose loans. Many DSCR lenders allow the borrower to be an LLC, and some also lend to corporations or partnerships.
- **Portfolio and commercial loans:** Banks that keep loans on their own books may lend to entities, often with different terms.

## Why Do Investors Buy Rentals in an LLC?

Investors often choose an LLC to:

- Separate rental finances from personal accounts
- Keep properties organized as the portfolio grows
- Bring in partners with clear ownership percentages
- Seek liability protection, depending on state law and how the LLC is run

An LLC isn't right for everyone, and the protection it offers depends on many factors. This article isn't legal or tax advice. Talk to a real estate attorney and a CPA before you form an entity.

## What Documents Do DSCR Lenders Ask for From an LLC?

Requirements vary by lender, but expect to provide:

1. **Articles of organization** (or certificate of formation) filed with the state
2. **Operating agreement** showing members, ownership percentages, and who can sign
3. **EIN confirmation letter** from the IRS
4. **Certificate of good standing** from the state, usually dated recently
5. **Signing authority**, such as a resolution or the operating agreement's management section
6. **Personal ID and credit** for each guarantor
7. **Foreign registration** if the LLC was formed in one state and the property is in another, when the property's state requires it

Many lenders want the LLC to be a single-purpose or small entity used for real estate. Some set a minimum ownership percentage for guarantors.

## Do I Have to Personally Guarantee an LLC DSCR Loan?

Usually, yes. A **personal guarantee** means you promise to repay the debt if the LLC can't. Lenders typically require guarantees from members who own a meaningful share of the company. Each guarantor's credit score is often used for pricing, so a partner's lower score can affect your rate.

Some lenders offer non-recourse or limited-guarantee options, but they're less common and may come with stricter terms.

## Can I Transfer a Rental I Already Own Into an LLC?

You can, but be careful. Most mortgages include a **due-on-sale clause**, which lets the lender call the loan due if the property is transferred. Federal law (the Garn-St Germain Act) protects certain transfers, such as to a living trust where you stay a beneficiary, but transfers to an LLC aren't on that protected list.

Common approaches include:

- **Asking your current lender** whether it will consent to the transfer
- **Refinancing into a DSCR loan in the LLC's name**, which pays off the old loan
- **Doing a DSCR cash-out refinance** to pull equity at the same time. See our [DSCR cash-out refinance and BRRRR guide](/posts/dscr-cash-out-refinance-brrrr/).

Ask about **seasoning**, which is how long the LLC or you must have owned the property before a cash-out refinance. Rules vary by lender.

## Should I Set Up My LLC Before Year-End?

There's no universal deadline, but there are reasons to plan now:

- **State filings take time**, and offices may close for the holidays.
- **Banking and insurance** should be in the LLC's name before closing.
- **1031 exchange timing:** If you're selling a rental and buying another, the replacement property usually needs to be titled the same way as the property you sold. Read our [year-end 1031 exchange guide](/posts/dscr-loan-1031-exchange-year-end/) and talk to your qualified intermediary.
- **Tax planning:** Your CPA can tell you whether forming or adding an entity this year or next makes more sense.

## What Mistakes Should LLC Investors Avoid?

- Signing a purchase contract in your personal name when you want to close in the LLC, without an assignment clause your lender accepts
- Letting your LLC fall out of good standing by missing annual reports or fees
- Mixing personal and LLC funds for down payments without a clear paper trail
- Forgetting that the property's insurance must name the LLC correctly
- Assuming every lender treats multi-member LLCs the same way

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can an LLC get a DSCR loan?

Yes. Many DSCR lenders allow an LLC to be the borrower on an investment property, usually with a personal guarantee from the members.

### Can an LLC get a conventional mortgage?

Generally no. Fannie Mae's Selling Guide lists individuals as eligible borrowers, with limited exceptions such as certain revocable trusts, so LLCs typically use DSCR, portfolio or commercial loans.

### What documents do I need for an LLC DSCR loan?

Lenders typically ask for articles of organization, an operating agreement, an EIN letter, a certificate of good standing and ID and credit for each guarantor.

### Do I need a personal guarantee for an LLC DSCR loan?

Usually, yes. Most DSCR lenders require a personal guarantee from members who own a meaningful share of the LLC, though requirements vary.

### Can I move my rental property into an LLC without refinancing?

Possibly, but a transfer can trigger the due-on-sale clause in your mortgage. Ask your lender for consent or refinance into a loan in the LLC's name, and consult an attorney.
