**An interest-only DSCR loan lets a real estate investor pay only interest for an initial period, often up to 10 years, before principal payments begin. The lower payment can raise your debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) and monthly cash flow. The tradeoff is that you build no equity through payments, and the payment rises when the interest-only period ends.**

As investors plan for 2027, many are looking for ways to keep rentals cash-flowing while rates stay high. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed average was 7.22% on November 4, 2026, and investor loans usually price above that. Here's how interest-only DSCR loans work, when they help, and what to watch for.

## Key Takeaways

- **Interest-only (IO) DSCR loans** charge only interest for a set period, then switch to principal and interest for the rest of the term.
- **Lower payment, higher ratio.** In our example below, IO raises the DSCR from 1.02 to 1.12 on the same rent.
- **The payment jumps later.** When the IO period ends, the remaining balance is paid off over a shorter period.
- **Pricing varies.** IO is often offered with higher credit score minimums, lower loan-to-value limits, or a higher rate than a fully amortizing DSCR loan.
- **Some lenders qualify you on the IO payment,** and others use the fully amortizing payment. Ask before you apply.

## How Does an Interest-Only DSCR Loan Work?

A DSCR loan qualifies you mainly on the property's rental income instead of your personal income. With an interest-only version, your payment for the first several years covers only the interest. A common structure is a 30-year loan with 10 years of interest-only payments, followed by 20 years of principal and interest.

The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-an-interest-only-loan-en-101/) explains that with an interest-only loan, your payments don't reduce what you owe during the IO period, and your payment increases once you start paying principal.

## How Does Interest-Only Change Your DSCR?

DSCR is gross monthly rent divided by the property's monthly housing payment, called PITIA (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and association dues). If you're new to the formula, see [how to calculate DSCR](/posts/how-to-calculate-dscr/).

Here's an illustrative example with a $300,000 loan at a hypothetical 7.50% rate (not a quote), $2,600 in monthly rent, and $450 per month for taxes, insurance, and HOA:

| | Fully amortizing (30 years) | Interest-only (first 10 years) |
|---|---|---|
| Principal and interest | $2,097.64 | $1,875.00 |
| Taxes, insurance, HOA | $450.00 | $450.00 |
| Total PITIA | $2,547.64 | $2,325.00 |
| DSCR ($2,600 rent) | 1.02 | 1.12 |
| Monthly cash flow before other costs | About $52 | About $275 |

The IO structure adds about $222 per month of cash flow in this example. For a lender that requires a 1.0 or 1.1 minimum ratio, that difference could decide whether the loan is approved.

## What Happens When the Interest-Only Period Ends?

In our example, after 10 years you would still owe the full $300,000. The loan then amortizes over the remaining 20 years, so principal and interest rise to about **$2,416.78 per month**. That's about $542 more than the IO payment, and about $319 more than the fully amortizing payment you could have had from day one.

Many investors plan ahead by:

- **Raising rents** over time so the property can carry the higher payment
- **Refinancing** before the IO period ends, which depends on future rates and values
- **Selling** the property, often as part of a longer-term plan
- **Making extra principal payments** during the IO period when cash flow allows

Watch for prepayment penalties if you plan to refinance or sell early. Our [DSCR prepayment penalty guide](/posts/dscr-loan-prepayment-penalty/) explains common structures.

## Who Is an Interest-Only DSCR Loan Best For?

IO can fit investors who:

- Want maximum monthly cash flow to build reserves or buy the next property
- Own properties in higher-priced markets where rents barely cover a fully amortizing payment
- Plan to hold for a shorter period, or to refinance after improving a property
- Have strong reserves to handle the payment increase later

IO may not fit investors who want to build equity steadily, plan to hold for 20+ years without refinancing, or are already stretched on reserves.

## What Do Lenders Require for Interest-Only DSCR Loans?

Requirements vary by lender, but IO options are often tied to stronger files. Common factors include a higher minimum credit score, a lower maximum loan-to-value, more reserves, and a minimum DSCR. Our [DSCR loan requirements guide](/posts/dscr-loan-requirements/) covers the typical ranges. Because DSCR loans are for business-purpose investment properties, they aren't available for a home you live in.

## Is Interest-Only Worth It Heading Into 2027?

It depends on your plan. With rates well above 2025 levels, IO can make a deal pencil out today. But it's a tool, not a shortcut. Run the numbers for both the IO period and the years after it, and compare them with a fully amortizing option before you choose.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is an interest-only DSCR loan?

It's a rental property loan qualified mainly on the property's rental income, where you pay only interest for an initial period, often up to 10 years. After that, the payment rises to include principal for the rest of the term.

### Does interest-only improve my DSCR?

Often, yes, if your lender calculates the ratio on the interest-only payment. A lower payment raises the ratio. In our example, the DSCR rose from 1.02 to 1.12 on the same rent. Some lenders use the fully amortizing payment instead.

### What happens when the interest-only period ends?

The full balance then amortizes over the remaining term, so the payment rises. On a $300,000 loan at 7.50%, the payment would rise from $1,875 interest-only to about $2,417 for the last 20 years of a 30-year loan.

### Are interest-only DSCR rates higher?

They can be. Many lenders price interest-only options higher or require a higher credit score, a lower loan-to-value or more reserves. Terms vary, so compare quotes for both interest-only and fully amortizing options.

### Can I refinance before the interest-only period ends?

Usually, yes, but it depends on future rates, property values and your loan's prepayment penalty. Check the prepayment terms before you sign if you might refinance or sell early.
