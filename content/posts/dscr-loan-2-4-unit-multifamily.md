**A DSCR loan can finance a duplex, triplex, or fourplex based on the property's rental income instead of your personal income. Lenders compare the combined rent from all units with the monthly payment. Typical industry guidelines call for 20% to 25% down on 2-4 unit rentals and a ratio of about 1.0 to 1.25 or higher.**

Small multifamily properties are a favorite for investors because several rents share one roof, one insurance policy, and one loan. Here's how DSCR financing typically works for two to four units, and where it differs from single-family rentals.

## Key Takeaways

- **Rent qualifies the loan,** not your W-2s or tax returns. The ratio is total rent divided by PITIA (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and association dues).
- **Typical down payments run 20% to 25%** for 2-4 units. Some lenders require about 5% more than for a single-family rental.
- **The appraisal uses Fannie Mae Form 1025,** the small residential income property report, which includes a rent analysis for each unit.
- **Five or more units** is generally a commercial loan, with different underwriting.
- **Terms vary by lender.** The ranges here are typical industry ranges, not Coastal Funding program terms.

## How Does a DSCR Loan Work for a Duplex or Fourplex?

A debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) loan is a business-purpose loan for investment properties. Instead of verifying your personal income, the lender asks one main question: does the property's rent cover its payment?

For a 2-4 unit property, the lender adds up the rent for every unit. It then divides that total by the monthly PITIA. A result of 1.0 means the rent exactly covers the payment. A result of 1.25 means the rent is 25% higher than the payment. For a refresher on the formula, see [how to calculate DSCR](/posts/how-to-calculate-dscr/).

## How Do Lenders Count Rent on 2-4 Units?

The appraiser completes [Fannie Mae Form 1025](https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/media/document/pdf/form-1025), the Small Residential Income Property Appraisal Report. It includes comparable rents for each unit and an estimate of market rent. Lenders typically:

- **Use current leases** for occupied units, often checked against market rent.
- **Use market rent** from the appraisal for vacant units, or when there's no lease.
- **Take the lower** of lease rent or market rent in some cases, depending on the lender.

If one unit is vacant at closing, ask early how the lender will treat it. Some lenders use full market rent, while others reduce it or want a signed lease.

## What Are Typical DSCR Requirements for 2-4 Units?

These are **typical industry ranges** and vary by lender and property:

| Factor | Typical range for 2-4 units |
|---|---|
| Down payment (purchase) | 20% to 25% |
| Minimum DSCR | About 1.0 to 1.25 (some lenders allow lower with tighter terms) |
| Credit score | Often 660 to 700+ for the best pricing |
| Reserves | Often 6 to 12 months of PITIA |
| Ownership | Personal name or an LLC, depending on the lender |
| Property | Non-owner-occupied investment property only |

For the full checklist, read [DSCR loan requirements](/posts/dscr-loan-requirements/).

## What Does a Fourplex DSCR Example Look Like?

Here's an illustration, not a quote. An investor buys a fourplex for $700,000 with 25% down:

| Item | Monthly amount |
|---|---|
| Loan: $525,000 at an illustrative 7.5%, 30 years (P&I) | About $3,671 |
| Property taxes (example) | $700 |
| Insurance (example) | $350 |
| **Total PITIA** | **About $4,721** |
| Rent: 4 units x $1,550 | $6,200 |
| **DSCR** | **About 1.31** |

At 1.31, this property would meet many lenders' typical minimums. If rents were $1,250 per unit ($5,000 total), the ratio would drop to about 1.06. That might still qualify with some lenders, but often with a higher rate or larger down payment.

## How Do 2-4 Unit Loan Sizes Compare to Conforming Limits?

For context, FHFA's 2026 baseline [conforming loan limits](https://www.fhfa.gov/news/news-release/fhfa-announces-conforming-loan-limit-values-for-2026) are $832,750 for one unit, $1,066,250 for two, $1,288,800 for three, and $1,601,750 for four. DSCR loans are non-QM loans, so they aren't bound by these limits. Many DSCR lenders still set their own maximum loan amounts, often well above these figures.

## What Changes at Five or More Units?

Properties with five or more units are generally considered commercial real estate. Those loans usually use a commercial appraisal, look at the property's net operating income, and may carry shorter terms or balloon payments. If you're deciding between a fourplex and a small apartment building, the financing path is a big part of the decision.

## Can You Refinance a 2-4 Unit Rental With a DSCR Loan?

Yes. Many investors buy or rehab a small multifamily property, lease the units, and then refinance with a DSCR loan to pull out equity. Cash-out maximums are typically lower than purchase limits, often around 70% to 75% of value. See our guide to [DSCR cash-out refinance and BRRRR](/posts/dscr-cash-out-refinance-brrrr/).

## Tips for Financing a Small Multifamily Rental

- **Collect leases and rent rolls** for every unit before you apply.
- **Get insurance quotes early.** Multifamily coverage can change your DSCR more than you expect.
- **Budget for separate utilities and maintenance,** and confirm who pays which utilities.
- **Compare several lenders,** since rent rules, vacancy treatment, and pricing vary widely.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use a DSCR loan for a duplex or fourplex?

Yes. Many DSCR lenders finance 2-4 unit investment properties. They add up the rent from all units and compare it with the monthly PITIA payment.

### How much down do I need for a DSCR loan on a 2-4 unit property?

Typical industry guidelines call for 20% to 25% down on a purchase. Terms vary by lender, credit score, and the property's DSCR.

### What DSCR do lenders require for multifamily properties?

Many lenders look for a DSCR of about 1.0 to 1.25 or higher. Some allow lower ratios with a higher rate or larger down payment.

### Can I live in one unit and use a DSCR loan?

No. DSCR loans are for business-purpose investment properties only. If you plan to live in one unit, ask about owner-occupied loan options instead.

### Is a five-unit property eligible for a DSCR loan?

Properties with five or more units are generally financed with commercial loans, which use different appraisals and underwriting than 1-4 unit DSCR loans.
