**DSCR loan requirements usually include a credit score of about 620 to 700+, a 20% to 25% down payment, a debt service coverage ratio near 1.0 or higher, and 3 to 6 months of reserves. Instead of your personal income, the lender qualifies the loan on the rental property's income. Exact rules vary by lender.**

Below is a plain-English checklist of what DSCR lenders typically look for, with real examples from published 2026 lender guidelines.

## Key Takeaways

- **Credit:** Many programs start at **620 to 660**. Scores of **700+** usually get the highest loan-to-value.
- **Down payment:** Typically **20% to 25%** on a purchase. Some strong files reach 80% LTV.
- **DSCR:** **1.0 or higher** is the common target. Some lenders allow lower ratios with tighter terms.
- **Reserves:** Often **3 to 6 months of PITIA**, more on larger loans.
- **No personal income docs:** Rent, not your W-2, drives qualification.
- **Investment only:** DSCR loans are business-purpose loans for non-owner-occupied rentals.

## What Is a DSCR Loan?

A **DSCR loan** is an investment-property mortgage that qualifies based on the **debt service coverage ratio**: the property's monthly rent divided by its monthly payment. That payment is called **PITIA**: principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and any HOA dues.

A DSCR of **1.0** means the rent exactly covers the payment. A **1.25** means rent is 25% higher than the payment. Need help with the math? See our step-by-step guide on [how to calculate DSCR](/posts/how-to-calculate-dscr/).

DSCR loans are **non-QM** (non-qualified mortgage) loans. They're made by private and wholesale lenders, not by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, so each lender sets its own guidelines.

## What Credit Score Do You Need for a DSCR Loan?

Most DSCR lenders set a floor somewhere between **620 and 660**. Your score then decides how much you can borrow:

- One national wholesale lender's DSCR matrix, updated **Oct. 1, 2026**, allows scores as low as **620**, but caps those loans at **65% LTV** and $1 million. A **700+** score opens up **80% LTV** on loans up to $1.5 million.
- The same matrix requires a **680** score when the DSCR is below 1.0.
- Another national lender's matrix (effective Feb. 25, 2026) starts at **640** for loans up to $500,000.

Lenders also look at **credit events**. For example, the October 2026 matrix above requires **48 months** after a bankruptcy, foreclosure, or short sale.

## How Much Down Payment Does a DSCR Loan Require?

Plan on **20% to 25% down** for most DSCR purchases, which equals a **75% to 80% loan-to-value (LTV)**. LTV is the loan amount divided by the property value.

You may need **more** down if:

- Your credit score is under 680 to 700
- The DSCR is **below 1.0**
- The property is a **condo** or condotel
- The loan is **large** (often above $1.5 to $2 million)
- It's a **cash-out refinance**, which usually caps LTV lower than a purchase

Many programs also allow **gift funds** with a minimum borrower contribution, and some accept **1031 exchange** funds. Rules vary.

## What Is the Minimum DSCR to Qualify?

The most common target is **1.0 or higher**. Many lenders give their best pricing at **1.20 to 1.25** or above.

Some programs are more flexible. The October 2026 matrix above has **no minimum DSCR** for most property types (condos need 1.0), and the February 2026 matrix allows ratios down to **0.80** with a 700 score and 70% max LTV. Lower ratios almost always mean a higher rate, more down, or both.

Today's rates matter here. Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed average was **7.32%** on Oct. 22, 2026, versus 6.19% a year earlier ([Freddie Mac](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)). DSCR loans are priced differently than that conventional benchmark, but higher rates raise PITIA and lower your DSCR on the same rent. ## How Many Months of Reserves Do DSCR Lenders Require?

**Reserves** are cash or liquid assets left over after closing, measured in months of PITIA. Typical requirements from the two 2026 matrices we reviewed:

| Loan amount | Oct. 2026 matrix | Feb. 2026 matrix |
|---|---|---|
| Up to $1 million | 3 months | 3 months (up to $1.5M) |
| $1M to $2M | 6 months | 6 months (over $1.5M) |
| Over $2M | 12 months | 9 months |

Some lenders let **cash-out proceeds** count toward reserves, and some require extra reserves for first-time investors. Retirement and brokerage accounts often count, sometimes at a discount.

## What Documents Do You Need for a DSCR Loan?

DSCR loans skip tax returns and pay stubs, but you'll still provide:

1. **Credit report** and photo ID
2. **Bank or asset statements** for down payment, closing costs, and reserves
3. **Appraisal with a rent schedule**: Fannie Mae **Form 1007** for single-family rentals or **Form 1025** for 2 to 4 units estimates market rent ([Fannie Mae Form 1007](https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/media/document/pdf/form-1007))
4. **Lease agreement**, if the property is already rented
5. **Entity documents** (articles, operating agreement, EIN) if closing in an LLC
6. **Insurance quote**, including flood coverage if required
7. **Mortgage history** on other properties, often with no 30-day lates in the last 12 months

Some lenders also limit points and fees, and many set **minimum loan amounts**, commonly **$75,000 to $150,000**.

## Which Properties and Borrowers Qualify?

**Eligible properties** typically include single-family homes, townhomes, PUDs, **2 to 4 unit** buildings, and warrantable condos. Some lenders accept non-warrantable condos, condotels, and rural homes with tighter limits. **Short-term rentals** are allowed by some lenders and excluded by others; see our [DSCR short-term rental guide](/posts/dscr-loan-short-term-rental/).

**Eligible borrowers** often include U.S. citizens, permanent residents, and in some programs foreign nationals or ITIN borrowers. Many lenders let you **close in an LLC or corporation** with personal guarantees from the owners.

**Not eligible:** a home you or your family will live in. DSCR loans are for business-purpose rentals only.

## Are There Other Terms to Watch?

- **Prepayment penalties:** Common on DSCR loans, often 1 to 5 years, though some states restrict or ban them.
- **Cash-out seasoning:** Many lenders want **6 months** of ownership before a cash-out refinance at today's value.
- **Interest-only options:** Available with some lenders, usually with a higher credit score.
- **Investor activity:** NAR reported that individual investors and second-home buyers made up **20%** of existing-home sales in September 2026, up from 15% a year earlier ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/existing-home-sales-increased-1-5-in-september-according-to-nar-report)).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What credit score do you need for a DSCR loan?

Many DSCR programs start around 620 to 660, and the best leverage and pricing usually go to scores of 700 or higher. Minimums vary by lender, loan size and loan-to-value.

### How much down payment does a DSCR loan require?

Most DSCR purchases need 20% to 25% down, which is a 75% to 80% loan-to-value. Lower scores, condos, larger loans, or a DSCR below 1.0 often require more.

### What is the minimum DSCR to qualify?

Many lenders look for a DSCR of 1.0 or higher, meaning rent covers the full payment. Some programs allow ratios below 1.0, or no minimum, with a higher credit score and a larger down payment.

### Do DSCR loans require tax returns or proof of income?

Usually not. DSCR loans qualify on the property's rental income compared with its payment, so personal tax returns, W-2s and pay stubs are typically not required. Lenders still check credit, assets and reserves.

### How many months of reserves do DSCR lenders want?

A common range is 3 to 6 months of PITIA for loans up to about $1.5 million, with more required for larger loans. Some lenders let cash-out proceeds count toward reserves.
